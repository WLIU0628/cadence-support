Support

Cadence is a free interval-timer app for iOS. The full feature set is unlocked on first launch — there is no Pro version, no paywall, no subscription.

Common questions

My Bluetooth strap isn't showing up.

Make sure HR Broadcast is enabled in the strap's companion app (WHOOP, Polar, Wahoo, Garmin). WHOOP straps in particular require the broadcast mode to be toggled on for each session — the app on your phone has a "Broadcast" button.

Then in Cadence, open Settings → Heart rate sensor → Scan. Stay within a few meters of the strap. After 30 seconds of no result, Cadence will offer a Scan-again button with a reminder.

My workout isn't appearing in Apple Health.

Open the iOS Settings app → Privacy & Security → Health → Cadence and confirm "Workouts" is enabled for write access. If it was previously denied, toggle it on there.

Inside Cadence, navigate to the run's History detail and tap Retry on the sync banner. The workout will re-attempt the Apple Health write.

The Live Activity isn't showing on my Lock Screen.

Open Settings → Face ID & Passcode → scroll to "Allow access when locked" and confirm Live Activities is on. Inside the iOS Settings app, also check Cadence → Live Activities is enabled.

The Lock Screen banner appears as soon as the workout timer starts (after the 3-2-1 preroll). If iOS has been heavily throttling activities recently due to background load, an already-running activity may be hidden — locking and unlocking the device usually surfaces it again.

Live HR isn't appearing during a workout.

Cadence needs an HR source. There are two options:

  • An Apple Watch paired with your iPhone that's feeding heart-rate samples to the Health app, OR
  • A Bluetooth heart-rate strap paired via Cadence's Settings.

The iPhone alone doesn't have a heart-rate sensor. If neither source is available, the in-app chip stays hidden — that's the expected behavior, not a bug.

How do I edit a seeded protocol like Norwegian 4×4?

In the Workouts tab, tap on the library row. The editor opens and everything is editable — names, durations, colors, zones. Your edits become a custom workout; the original seed stays in the library unchanged.

To restore a seeded protocol you've edited, you can delete it from the list — but note: a deleted seed stays deleted (won't reappear on next launch). If you change your mind, the simplest path is to reinstall the app (your run history is in Apple Health if you've been writing workouts).

What zone model should I pick?

%HRmax — simplest. Uses just your max HR. Norwegian 4×4 was originally defined in %HRmax (Wisløff's paper).

Karvonen (%HRR) — uses max HR and resting HR. More personal: at the same effort fraction, a runner with low resting HR gets a higher target BPM than one with high resting HR. If you know both numbers and want more accurate zones, this is the pick.

%LTHR — uses your lactate-threshold HR. Best for runners who have done a recent lactate test or threshold workout and know that number. Skip if you don't.

How do I turn off the spoken or haptic cues?

Two layers:

  • Per workout: in the workout editor, the "Coaching cues" section has separate toggles for haptic and TTS. Both are off by default.
  • Global: in Cadence Settings, there are master toggles for haptic and TTS — turning these off disables cues everywhere regardless of per-workout settings.
